The Story of Karna and the Armor

Princess Kunti, before her marriage, received a sacred mantra that allowed her to summon any god and bear his child. Curious and afraid, she invoked Surya, the Sun God. From that union, Karna was born—radiant like the sun itself. But society was cruel. Terrified of shame, Kunti placed the newborn in a basket and set him afloat on the river Ganga. Fate carried the child to a charioteer, Adhiratha, and his wife Radha, who raised Karna as their own.

As the Kurukshetra war drew near, Indra, the king of the gods and father of Arjuna, feared Karna’s strength. Disguised as a poor Brahmin, Indra approached Karna and asked him for his armor and earrings as alms. Karna immediately recognized Indra and understood that giving away the armor would make him mortal. Even so, he chose to honor his promise of generosity. Without hesitation, Karna cut the armor and earrings from his own body and gave them away. Moved by Karna’s sacrifice, Indra granted him a boon in return: the Vasavi Shakti, a powerful divine weapon that could kill any enemy—but could be used only once. Later, during the war, Karna used the Vasavi Shakti to defeat Ghatotkacha, not knowing that this would leave him defenseless. In his final battle against Arjuna, Karna fought without his divine armor. When his chariot wheel became stuck in the ground, he was struck down.

Motu and Patlu
