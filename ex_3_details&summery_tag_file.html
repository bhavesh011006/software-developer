gujarati recipe
Dhokla

1 cup gram flour besan
1 tbsp semolina (optional, for texture)
1 tbsp lemon juice
1 tsp sugar
1 tsp ginger-green chili paste
1 tsp Eno fruit salt (or ½ tsp baking soda)
Salt to taste
¾ cup water

Mix besan, semolina, salt, sugar, ginger-chili paste, lemon juice, and water to make a smooth batter. Grease a steaming plate and preheat steamer for 5 minutes. Add Eno to batter, mix gently until frothy. Pour into plate and steam for 15–20 minutes. For tempering, heat oil, add mustard seeds, sesame seeds, curry leaves, chilies. Add sugar-water-lemon mix and boil for 1 minute. Pour tempering over dhokla and cut into squares.

south indian
Idli

Ingredients
2 cups idli rice (or parboiled rice)
1 cup urad dal (split, skinless black gram)
½ tsp fenugreek seeds (methi seeds)
Salt to taste
Water as needed
Oil (for greasing molds)

Idli is a soft, steamed South Indian savory cake made primarily from rice and urad dal (skinned black gram). To prepare traditional idli, soak 2 cups of parboiled idli rice and 1 cup of split urad dal separately in water for about 4–6 hours; you can also add 1 teaspoon of fenugreek seeds to the dal while soaking to enhance fermentation and flavor. After soaking, drain the water and grind the urad dal first with a little water until it becomes light and fluffy, then grind the rice to a slightly coarse, smooth batter. Mix both batters together in a large bowl, add salt to taste, and stir well to incorporate air. Cover and allow the batter to ferment in a warm place for 8–12 hours or overnight, until it rises and becomes airy. Once fermented, gently stir the batter, grease idli molds lightly with oil, and pour the batter into the molds without overfilling. Steam the idlis in an idli steamer or pressure cooker (without the whistle) for about 10–12 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted comes out clean. Let them rest for a couple of minutes before removing with a spoon. Idlis are best served hot with coconut chutney and sambar, making them a light, healthy, and easily digestible breakfast option.
Rajasthani dish recipe
daal bati

Dal Baati is a traditional Rajasthani dish from Rajasthan, known for its rich flavors and rustic preparation. To make it, first prepare the baati by mixing whole wheat flour with semolina, salt, a pinch of baking soda, and generous amounts of ghee; knead it into a stiff dough using water, shape it into round balls, and bake them in a preheated oven at about 180°C (or traditionally over charcoal) until golden and crisp on the outside, then dip the hot baatis in melted ghee. For the dal, combine soaked toor dal, moong dal, and chana dal, pressure cook them with turmeric and salt until soft, then temper with ghee heated with cumin seeds, mustard seeds, asafoetida, garlic, ginger, green chilies, and dry red chilies; add chopped tomatoes, coriander powder, red chili powder, and garam masala, cook until aromatic, then mix this tempering into the cooked dal and simmer until well blended. Garnish with fresh coriander leaves and serve the hot, ghee-soaked baatis broken into pieces alongside the flavorful dal, often accompanied by churma, onions, and lemon for an authentic experience.
